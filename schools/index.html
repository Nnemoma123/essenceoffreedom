What Is Ndidi Breathwork™

Ndidi (n-DEE-dee) Breathwork™ is African-centered, not a borrowed wellness trend. The sessions are guided by breath, sound, and movement with live African instruments and music.

Each session releases stored stress and clears mental fog, so your students and staff bring more energy, clarity, and focus to their day.

Ndidi means steadfastness in Igbo, the patient endurance to keep returning to your body, breath by breath, until what has been stored there finally moves and you feel alive again.

Ndidi Breathwork™ is built around three pillars, which can surface once the body lets go of what it has been holding:

Udo Peace

Harmony, rest, inner peace

Ike Strength

Inner power, vitality, agency

Aṅụrị Joy

Delight, laughter, aliveness

I am free. I am alive. Energy flows through me.

Meet Your Guide

Nnemoma Chukwumerije, LMSW — African-Centered Breathwork Guide & Life Coach

Nnemoma holds her LMSW from Columbia University and brings 8+ years of youth development experience, including three years in school-based suicide prevention with the JED Foundation.

She's also a Nigerian medicine woman and life coach, using breath, sound, and movement, rooted in her Nigerian ancestry, to help people release what's blocking them and access the clarity, energy, and vitality to pursue their goals.

Her path here started in a cubicle, not a wellness space. As the first-born daughter of Nigerian immigrants, she did everything "right," straight As, a stable career in nonprofit, and still found herself depressed, living a zombie-like existence. Breathwork changed that. What began as a personal practice became a calling, and now she brings this healing work to schools and college campuses nationwide, for the students carrying more than they should, and the staff holding space for them.

What A Session Looks Like

Every session is adapted in real time to the room, the group, and the moment, whether we're together in person or connecting virtually.

01

Breathwork

The foundation: guided breathing patterns that shift the nervous system out of fight-or-flight and into a state where real stress release becomes possible.

02

Sound

Live instruments and music woven with the breath to deepen the experience and help the body release what words can't reach.

03

Movement

Gentle physical practice, stretching, swaying, simple postures, that brings students and staff back into their bodies before and after the deeper breathwork.

04

Community

Practiced in circle, not isolation. Participants breathe, move, and witness each other, often where the real shift happens.

05

Reflection

A closing sharing prompt that turns what surfaced in the body into language, clarity, and an intention to carry forward.

FAQ

Is it safe for anxious or trauma-affected students?

Yes — sessions are trauma-informed and paced by the group, and participation in any part is always optional.

What equipment or space do we need?

A room where people can sit comfortably, chairs or floor, ideally in a circle. Depending on group size, audio support may be required for music.

What budget does this fit under?

This work fits directly into budgets many schools and colleges already have: Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) initiatives and MTSS/tiered support frameworks in K-12, and student wellness, belonging, and retention initiatives in higher ed. If your institution already funds mental health programming, professional development, or student life initiatives, Ndidi Breathwork™ fits into that line, not a new category to justify.

How do you handle parent/guardian consent for minors?

Nnemoma works with your team to communicate session content to parents and guardians in advance and to honor any opt-outs. Nothing is mandatory, every practice is offered as an invitation.

Is this religious?

No. It's rooted in Nigerian cultural tradition, not tied to any religious practice or belief system, and welcoming to people of all backgrounds.
